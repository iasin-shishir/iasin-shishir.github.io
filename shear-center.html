Torsional moments are caused by forces acting away from the longitudinal axis of a structural member. Every cross-section of a structural member possesses a particular point through which the transverse load must be applied in order to prevent the development of torsional moment in the section. This point is known as the Shear Center of the section.

When a transverse load is applied away from the shear center, it produces a torsional moment, causing the member to twist about the shear center. For this reason, the shear center is also known as the center of twist.

For cross-sections having one axis of symmetry (e.g., T-sections and channel sections), the shear center always lies on the axis of symmetry. For cross-sections having two axes of symmetry (e.g., rectangular, circular, and I-sections), the shear center coincides with the centroid of the cross-sectional area.

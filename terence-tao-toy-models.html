Toy Models and the Freedom to Fail

Mathematics

Terence Chi-Shen Tao, the 'Mozart of Math', said,

Mathematicians intentionally simplify reality. They work on what are called 'toy models'. Physicists call them 'spherical cow' type assumptions. When you want to model a cow, it’s actually easier for the physics if the cow is a complete sphere and frictionless. That’s not realistic, but it’s a good starting point. And then over time, you decide to add some friction, add some legs to the cow. But you start with the easiest cases to get some initial idea.

I guess the difference between math and the other science - basically anything else - is that we can change all our hypotheses and work with these toy cases. Like, you know, if you want to build a bridge and you’re an engineer, you can’t just build a toy bridge over a creek first and then float your way up to a real bridge...

In most professions, you don’t get to play your toy models because you’re not allowed to fail. But in math, failure is very, very cheap. You know, you try a problem, you don’t solve it, fine. You just toss the paper away and you try again. No one gets hurt, no one dies.
